## BLOQUE 2 — HAD + PARTICIPLE EN PAPEL (40') — DE PIE

**Objective: everyone forms the past perfect, places the earlier event in the right clause, and writes it without the base-verb or simple-past trap.**

1. **Tablero — cita del libro + regla (5'), DE PIE.** La clase es 100% ingles; el libro esta en ingles en esta pagina:
```
PAST PERFECT — HAD + past participle (B1 Book M9, Guide 2, p.103)
Book: "The past perfect tense uses the same participle, but the auxiliary
  verb 'to have' is in the past tense ('had')."
Book: "We use the past perfect together with the simple past to talk about
  events that happened before another past event."
Book: "The past perfect is a 'narrative tense' because it helps us explain
  the relationship in a story."
Book model: "When we moved to Colombia, I had learned Spanish."
HAD never changes: I / you / he / she / it / we / they + HAD + participle
(-) "had not" -> "hadn't"         (?) "Had you finished?" -> "Yes, I had."
Regular participle = -ed (A2 M15 p.134): finished · prepared · worked
Irregular (A2 M16 p.148): been · taken · driven · eaten · gone · told · found
Book examples (p.104): "I had studied English before I went to London."
  "When I ate dinner, I had already done my homework."
  "Arnulfo arrived early today because he had forgotten to shower."
  "I met my friend Chris after I had studied in Paris."
```
2. **Señala la logica (2'), DE PIE.** *"HAD carries the tense; the verb wears its PARTICIPLE. The book calls it a narrative tense: it is the floor under the story you told this morning."* Contraste en voz alta: *"When I got there, it started"* vs *"When I got there, it HAD started."*
3. **Drill en circulo, DE PIE (9').** Cadena cerrada. Tu lanzas 2 eventos ("arrive / the meeting start"); estudiante 1 dice cual fue primero; 2 los une con **before / after**; 3 los une con **because** o **when**; 4 hace la pregunta + short answer (*"Had the meeting started? — Yes, it had."*); 5 lanza el siguiente par. Velocidad creciente; la cadena no se detiene cuando alguien se cae.
4. **Errores tipicos al tablero (4'), coro 2x cada correccion:**
   - "I had finish" -> "I had FINISHED" (participio, nunca base)
   - "I had went" -> "I had GONE" (participio irregular, nunca pasado simple)
   - "I have studied before I went" -> "I HAD studied before I went" (auxiliar en pasado)
   - HAD en el evento equivocado -> HAD va en lo ANTERIOR, no en el principal
5. **Zone walk, DE PIE, en parejas (10').** Tablero en dos zonas: `MAIN EVENT (simple past)` | `EARLIER EVENT (had + participle)`. Dices dos hechos de trabajo ("the client called / I sent the quote"); cada pareja **CAMINA** a la zona de cada hecho **Y** dice en voz alta la frase unida con before / after / when / because. La pareja que se equivoca de zona la corrige la de la zona correcta, con la formula *"Almost — it's..."*. No tu.
6. **Ejemplos del libro al papel del estudiante (6'), DE PIE junto al tablero. Dictado, nada impreso.** Dictas los dos hechos en base y ellos escriben **la frase completa** marcando con una flecha cual fue primero. Clave en el ANEXO, no la pongas antes:
```
DICTATION — one sentence, the earlier event with HAD:
 1. I / study English  ->  I / go to London       (before)
 2. Bruno / say good night  ->  he / go to bed    (before)
 3. Arnulfo / arrive early  <-  he / forget to shower   (because)
 4. we / start the class  <-  Paola / arrive      (when: Paola arrived...)
```
7. **Cierre del bloque — produccion libre, de pie (4').** Cada estudiante escribe en su papel y dice en voz alta 2 frases verdaderas sobre un logro propio: una con *because* y otra con *by the time*. El drill nunca cierra el bloque: cierra el uso libre.
